# Àlááfìa — content playbook

**Cadence:** 2 posts/week (Tue + Fri), 9 Sep 2026 → 29 Jan 2027 = **21 weeks, 42 posts.**
**Channels:** LinkedIn + X on every post; Instagram (feed / carousel / story) on the visual-led ones.
**Design system:** `content/design/` canvas — direction picked, then the full template set. This doc is direction-agnostic.

---

## 1. Voice

Confident, sharp, a little provocative. We show, we don't hedge. Numbers with attitude, always with their source and their uncertainty. We are building the thing in the open — wins *and* the unglamorous parts.

- **Do:** "We can see Nigeria's health down to the ward. Here's what it looks like." Short sentences. One idea per post. Cite the source in-frame. Say the credible interval out loud.
- **Don't:** "empowering communities", "leveraging data for impact", "we're on a mission to". No corporate-NGO fog. No stat without a source. No claim the method can't defend.

## 4. Production workflow (per post)

1. Slot comes up on the calendar → pull the archetype + topic.
2. Data check: is the number/map defensible *today*? If not, swap the slot — never ship a stat the method can't stand behind.
3. Draft copy (hook + body + alt text + hashtags) in this doc under the week.
4. Build the visual from the template for that archetype, in the 3 sizes.
5. Grayscale + phone-width check.
6. Queue. Post Tue/Fri ~09:00 WAT (LinkedIn/IG) and ~14:00 WAT (X).

### 4 · HOW WE KNOW — why the average is wrong (carousel, 5)
**Hook:** Why "the national average" is usually the wrong number to act on. A thread in 5.
**Slides:**
1. **The national average is wrong to act on.** Here's why — and what to use instead.
2. **It hides the range.** A 32% national stunting rate can be 16% in one LGA and 51% in another. Acting on 32% helps neither.
3. **It's stale by design.** National surveys run every 5-ish years. A 2024 figure is your best guess for 2026 — fine for context, weak for this year's plan.
4. **It's the wrong grain.** Programmes run in LGAs and wards. Money moves at that level. The number has to match the decision.
5. **The fix:** model the survey down to small areas using satellite + routine-data covariates, show a credible interval on every estimate, and check it against the official state numbers. That's what we're building — follow along.

### 7 · ONE NUMBER — survey density
**Hook:** Nigeria's flagship health survey interviews ~40,000 women. Across 774 LGAs, that's about 50 each.
**Body:**
That's plenty to describe a state. It is not enough to hand you a direct, defensible number for a single LGA — let alone a ward, where it's closer to 4 people.

This is exactly where a lot of "ward-level data" quietly falls apart: it's a national survey sliced too thin and presented as fact.

The honest way to get a ward number is to *model* it — borrow strength from neighbouring areas and from satellite and routine-data covariates, and put a credible interval on the result. That's the method. We'll show our working every step.

### 8 · HOW WE KNOW — SAE in plain terms (carousel, 6)
**Hook:** Small-area estimation, explained without the stats degree. 6 slides.
**Slides:**
1. **The problem:** the survey is solid at state level, thin below it. You still need ward numbers.
2. **The idea:** places near each other, and places that look alike, tend to be alike. Use that.
3. **The ingredients:** the survey (the truth, where it's dense) + covariates that *are* measured everywhere — poverty surface, night lights, travel time, rainfall, schooling.
4. **The model:** predict the indicator from those covariates + a smooth spatial term, at the cluster level, respecting the survey design.
5. **The output:** an estimate for every ward — with a credible interval. Wide interval = "we're not sure here." That honesty is the point.
6. **The check:** add the ward estimates back up to the state level. They should match the official survey number. If they don't, the model's wrong, not the survey.

## 7. Standing rules

- **Never post a number the method can't defend.** If the map isn't ready or the estimate is too uncertain to show, run an evergreen (THESIS / HOW WE KNOW) instead.
- **Source line in every frame.** Survey + year, or "modelled — NDHS 2023-24 + covariates".
- **Show uncertainty.** At least a "wide interval here" note; ideally the interval itself in the replies.
- **No fabricated milestones.** BUILD LOG only reports what actually shipped.
- **Bracketed facts get checked** against the real figure before the post goes out.
- **State the coverage, not just the count.** Wards + facilities are 24 of 37 states (GRID3 v3.0). Any post citing 5,872 wards or 41,778 facilities says "across 24 states" in the same breath. The 13 missing — Akwa Ibom, Anambra, Benue, Cross River, Ebonyi, Edo, Ekiti, Imo, Lagos, Ondo, Plateau, Rivers, Taraba — have LGA + settlement geometry only.
